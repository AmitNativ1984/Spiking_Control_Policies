Projected onto the camera image

The same penalty painted across the actual 320×180 depth image, through the pinhole projection. Inside the image the cost runs from zero at the centre pixel to 1.0 λ at the edge midpoints — and 1.57 λ in the corners, because the corners sit at r_fov = 1.25, outside the inscribed ellipse despite being perfectly visible pixels. That is a real quirk of using an elliptical norm on a rectangular sensor; see the note below.

Notes

  • The corner quirk is real. `r_fov` uses an elliptical norm, so the image corners score 1.25 and are charged as if outside the cone even though they are visible pixels. It is the conservative direction to err in — corners are the worst part of a depth image — but if it matters, a rectangular (max-of-axes) norm would treat the whole image as free instead.
  • The crash overlay is vehicle-frame. The per-crash azimuth and elevation were logged in the yaw-only frame, while the penalty is computed in the body frame. Measured difference between the two is 2.6–3.2% on mean r_fov, so the overlay is a close but not exact match to what the term would have charged.
  • Speed is folded out of these plots. Everything shown assumes ‖v‖ ≥ v_ref = 2 m/s, where the speed multiplier saturates. Below that the whole surface scales linearly with speed, and a hover costs nothing at any angle.
