The atlas

Each panel is every collision in one run, binned by the body-frame direction of travel at impact: azimuth left-to-right, elevation bottom-to-top, boresight dead centre. Cells are a percentage of that run's crashes, on one shared scale, so the three panels are directly comparable. The dashed box is the real camera frustum (±43.5° × ±28.1°); the inscribed ellipse is r_fov = 1, the contour p_fov's penalty is anchored to. All three maps have the same shape: two lobes straddling boresight in azimuth, centred slightly below the horizon, with a long tail running out past ±90°. Adding a reward term changed how many crashes there were, not where they pointed.

02

Azimuth: the term that moved it, and the term that worked

This is the awkward result. p_blind moved the azimuth distribution most — median |azimuth| 43.9° → 32.0°, crashes with the obstacle behind the drone 24.8% → 16.4% — and its crash rate did not improve at all (18.1% vs b4's 17.8%). p_fov barely moved it (43.9° → 41.1°) and cut the crash rate by 32% relative. Whatever p_fov bought, it did not buy it by making the drone fly where the camera looks, which is the mechanism it was designed around. Both terms have a peak at zero azimuth that no term removes: flying straight ahead and hitting something straight ahead is a sizeable share of every run.

03

Elevation: nothing touched it

The vertical half-angle is 28.1°, tighter than the horizontal 43.5° because the sensor is 320×180. Across all three rewards the share of crashes whose velocity lay beyond it sits at 34–37% and median |elevation| at 19–21° — flat to within noise. p_blind excludes the vertical channel by construction (yaw cannot fix vertical blindness), and p_fov, which does include it, moved it 2pp in the wrong direction. The distribution also leans consistently below the horizon, which is where the floor and the trunks are.

04

Where the crashes sit on the penalty surface

Collapsing both angles into p_fov's own radial coordinate, r_fov = √((ψ/43.5°)² + (θ/28.1°)²), puts every crash on one axis: 1.0 is the frustum edge, and the penalty grows as r_fov² beyond it. The three curves are nearly superimposed. Median r_fov is 1.45 / 1.32 / 1.41 for b4 / p_blind / p_fov, and roughly two thirds of all crashes in every run happen with the velocity vector already outside the cone. A term that charges for exactly this quantity ran for 2,000 epochs and left the quantity where it found it.

What this implies for the next term

p_blind raised the aiming metric and bought nothing; p_fov bought a third of the crash rate without raising it. Together that is evidence the binding constraint is not where the camera points — it is how much room is left and how fast the gap is closing. Median impact distance to the struck obstacle is 1.11–1.20 m in all three runs and median impact speed 1.24–2.14 m/s: these are close-quarters, low-speed contacts in clutter, not high-speed runs into something off-axis. That is the case for a clearance term that couples speed to remaining distance rather than to direction.

Method

  • Each checkpoint's actor was rebuilt from its saved weights and run deterministically (no exploration noise, no gradient updates) for 6,000 physics steps across 256 parallel environments, with the curriculum pinned at min_level = max_level = 30 (analysis/crash_cause_eval.py).
  • A crash is the task's own collision buffer — a contact-force threshold on the airframe — firing outside an arrival or out-of-bounds event.
  • Azimuth is atan2(v_y, v_x) and elevation is atan2(v_z, |v_xy|) of the velocity in the body frame, the frame the camera is bolted to — so a nose-down attitude tilts the frustum with the airframe, exactly as p_fov sees it. This is the direction of travel, not the direction to the obstacle.
  • The vertical half-angle is not configured upstream; it follows from the 87° horizontal FOV and the 320×180 pixel aspect under the square-pixel pinhole assumption, which is standard but unverified against aerial_gym's actual projection.
  • b4's numbers here come from the re-run that added the azimuth/elevation fields, so its crash rate reads 17.8% against the 16.4% quoted in the first report — a different 6,000-step sample of the same checkpoint, not a different policy.
  • p_fov ran with lambda_fov = 0.015 and p_blind disabled; p_blind ran with lambda_blind active and r_bearing deleted. Both descend from b4, which still had r_bearing — so each comparison against b4 moves more than one term. Unavoidable given the lineage.
  • p_fov completed 8,128 episodes against b4's 10,097 in the same step budget: its episodes last longer, which is itself consistent with fewer early terminations.
