Obstacle type

Trees are not the problem. At 3% of the obstacle pool they cause only 1.5% of crashes — half their expected share. The disproportionate offender is panels: 3.5% of the pool but 8.2% of crashes, a 2.3× over-rate. Cylinders run slightly hot (1.2×); spheres, walls and the generic-clutter object class — upstream's largest single category at 47.5% of the pool, outside the five types originally asked about — land close to their base rate.

02

Spawn traps

206 crashes (12.5%) happen before step 5 — before the policy has flown far enough to see anything, let alone react. They cluster almost entirely on walls: a quarter of every wall collision (25.9%) is a spawn trap, with a median impact at step 25 overall for that type — roughly five times earlier than panels, spheres, cylinders or objects (median step 86–92). Trees have zero spawn traps; a canopy that size is never close enough at spawn to hit immediately.

03

Camera memory

This is the clearest signal in the log. Across the last 10 physics frames before impact, the obstacle that ended the episode was absent from every single frame in 85.8% of crashes — not partially occluded, not at the frame edge: fully out of the 87°×56° frustum the whole lookback window. Only 6.8% of crashes (113) had the obstacle in view for ≥90% of that window — those are genuine reactive-avoidance failures; everything else is a blind-spot collision. A policy with no state carried between frames cannot avoid what it stopped seeing several steps ago — the failure mode a GRU or a stacked-latent history is built to fix.

04

Motion vs. camera

Impact speed averages 1.49 m/s (median 1.20, max 5.47) — the policy is rarely at full tilt when it hits something. The angle between body-frame velocity and the camera boresight spreads wide (median 58.9°), with the single largest cluster at just 15–30° off-axis — a near head-on approach to a target the camera nonetheless failed to carry forward in memory. The two outliers by type: walls are hit almost perpendicular (86.8° mean) at the lowest speed (1.11 m/s) — the spawn-trap signature, sliding sideways into a nearby face before forward flight is established — and trees are hit more head-on (42.3°) and faster (2.46 m/s) than anything else, a rarer but more direct fly-through.

Method

  • The b4 checkpoint's actor was rebuilt directly from its saved weights and run deterministically (no exploration noise, no gradient updates) inside the live task for 6,000 physics steps across 256 parallel environments, with curriculum min_level = max_level = 30 pinned throughout — the density the training curriculum tops out at.
  • A crash is the task's own collision buffer (a contact-force threshold on the airframe) firing outside an arrival or out-of-bounds event — the same definition compute_rewards() uses to end an episode. 1,651 fired this way, for a 16.4% crash rate consistent with the ~17–21% plateau seen across training runs at this level.
  • The struck obstacle is the nearest live obstacle to the airframe at the moment of impact (this task exposes obstacle position but not a contact-body id, so two obstacles within a few tens of centimeters of each other could be misattributed).
  • "In view" is a geometric frustum + range check (87° horizontal / 56.1° vertical FOV, 0.1–10 m range) against the struck obstacle's static position at each of the last ≤10 physics steps ending at impact — it re-derives visibility from pose history, it does not re-sample the rendered depth image. It also uses the camera's nominal mount pose, ignoring the ≤12 cm translation offset and ±5° rotation randomization applied per environment at reset.
  • Vertical FOV (56.1°) isn't configured upstream; it's derived from the 87° horizontal FOV and the sensor's 320×180 pixel aspect ratio under a square-pixel assumption, not read directly from the simulator.
