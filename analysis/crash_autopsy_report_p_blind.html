01

Obstacle type

The ranking is unchanged from b4, and the standout offender got worse. Panels are 3.5% of the obstacle pool but 9.2% of crashes — a 2.63× over-rate, up from b4's already-disproportionate 2.34×. Cylinders again run hot (1.17×). The one real shift: trees moved from half their pool share under b4 (0.50×, 25 crashes) to 0.85× under p_blind (48 crashes) — still under-represented, but the absolute tree-crash count nearly doubled on a comparable episode count, consistent with the higher impact speeds below giving the policy less margin around large obstacles.

02

Spawn traps

Spawn traps overall are slightly less common than under b4 (10.9% vs 12.5%) — but they have concentrated even harder on walls. Under b4, a quarter of wall crashes (25.9%) happened before step 5; under p_blind it's half (50.0%), with a median wall-crash step of just 10 (down from 25). Whatever p_blind changed about early yaw/heading behavior, it made the first few steps riskier specifically around walls, even as the rest of the spawn-trap picture improved slightly.

03

Camera memory

Still the largest single factor, but measurably less dominant than under b4. 75.6% of crashes (down from 85.8%) hit an obstacle absent from all 10 prior frames, and the mean visible-fraction rose from 0.102 to 0.165. p_blind's reward term explicitly targets "motion the camera cannot see" — this is consistent with it nudging the policy to keep more obstacles in frame before it reaches them. It has not fixed the underlying issue: four in five crashes are still functionally blind, which is still the strongest argument in either run for adding real memory (a GRU or stacked-latent history) rather than more reward shaping on top of a memoryless MLP.

04

Motion vs. camera

This is where p_blind diverges most from b4. Median impact speed nearly doubled, from 1.20 to 2.14 m/s (max 9.11 m/s vs 5.47), while the angle off boresight barely moved (57.9° vs 58.9° median). p_blind is not crashing into different things at different angles — it is flying, and crashing, faster. That is the simplest explanation for why the memory picture improved (a faster-moving camera keeps more of the room passing through frame) while the overall crash rate still went up: the added speed outweighs the added visibility.

Method

  • Identical protocol to the b4 report: the checkpoint's actor was rebuilt directly from its saved weights and run deterministically (no exploration noise, no gradient updates) for 6,000 physics steps across 256 parallel environments, curriculum min_level = max_level = 30 pinned throughout.
  • Evaluated on the exact commit (4cd8490) the p_blind checkpoint was trained at, rather than the current tip, since the checkpoint's observation layout has to match the checked-out task code exactly. Verified unchanged from the b4-era code (17+32 = 49-D observation) — p_blind only changed the reward function.
  • A crash is the task's own collision buffer (a contact-force threshold on the airframe) firing outside an arrival or out-of-bounds event. 1,877 fired this way, for an 18.1% crash rate — close to the ~18-19% seen in this run's own training-time logs at level 30.
  • The struck obstacle is the nearest live obstacle to the airframe at the moment of impact (no contact-body id exists in this task, so two obstacles within a few tens of centimeters of each other could be misattributed).
  • "In view" is a geometric frustum + range check (87° horizontal / 56.1° vertical FOV, 0.1–10 m range, nominal un-jittered mount pose) against the struck obstacle's static position at each of the last ≤10 physics steps ending at impact.
  • Single seed per checkpoint on both sides of this comparison — the deltas above are a single training run each, not an average over a seed spread, so treat them as directional rather than statistically confirmed.
